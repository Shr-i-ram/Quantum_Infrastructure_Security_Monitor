## Slide 1: End-to-End Simulation Pipeline Architecture
- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/01_simulation_pipeline.png`
- **Slide Title:** *QISM Simulation Pipeline: Physics-Informed Stochastic QKD Telemetry*

### What It Shows
A four-stage architectural diagram detailing the end-to-end dataflow from Alice's state preparation through the optical fiber channel, Bob's SPAD detection apparatus, classical sifting, and telemetry aggregation into sharded Parquet datasets.

### What to Say
> *"Rather than using a simplified macroscopic model where QBER is directly sampled from a formula, QISM enforces a strict **causal propagation invariant**. Eavesdropper attacks and environmental noise perturb physical parameters (such as fiber attenuation, detector dead time, or pulse mean intensity). These interventions propagate forward into microscopic photon interactions, which produce detector clicks, which are subsequently aggregated into multi-rate telemetry. At no point is telemetry modified synthetically."*

### Why It Matters
Establishes the scientific legitimacy and credibility of the simulator: the generated datasets represent genuine physical telemetry emerging from quantum optical and detector interactions.

## Slide 2: Microscopic Event-Level Pulse Timeline
- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/02_event_timeline.png`
- **Slide Title:** *Event-Level Simulation: Pulse-by-Pulse Optical States & Detection*

### What It Shows
A microscopic event raster spanning 50 consecutive pulses. It tracks Alice's prepared bit ($0$ or $1$), basis ($Z$ or $X$), pulse intensity state (Signal, Decoy, Vacuum), sampled Poisson photon number ($n \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$), Bob's chosen basis, detector click outcomes (D0, D1, double clicks, dark counts), and the resulting sifted key status.

### What to Say
> *"Here we see how individual optical pulses behave inside the simulation engine. Alice modulates weak coherent pulses where photon numbers follow Poisson statistics. When Alice and Bob happen to choose matching measurement bases (Z and Z, or X and X) and a photon triggers an avalanche click in one detector, that event is retained as a sifted key bit. Notice that vacuum and decoy pulses sample different photon numbers, providing the foundation for decoy-state security."*

### Why It Matters
Visually demonstrates that the simulator operates at the fundamental pulse level, sampling actual photon numbers and detector states rather than estimating key rates analytically.

## Slide 3: Quantum Channel Attenuation & Noise Floor
- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/03_channel_transmission_and_loss.png`
- **Slide Title:** *Channel Modeling: Beer-Lambert Attenuation & Dark Count Floor*

### What It Shows
1. **Left Panel:** Channel transmittance $T(L) = 10^{-(\alpha L + L_{\mathrm{sys}})/10}$ plotted on a logarithmic scale for standard fiber attenuation coefficients ($\alpha = 0.18, 0.20, 0.25\,\text{dB/km}$), with empirical scenario instances overlaid.
2. **Right Panel:** Expected detection rate $R_{\mathrm{det}}(L) = f_{\mathrm{rep}} \mu T(L) \eta_{\mathrm{det}} + 2 R_{\mathrm{dark}}$ decaying exponentially before hitting the constant thermal dark-count noise floor.

### What to Say
> *"In quantum communications, photon survival drops exponentially with distance according to the Beer-Lambert law. As fiber length increases beyond 30 to 40 kilometers, true signal detections drop below the detector's intrinsic dark-count rate. Because dark counts occur randomly across D0 and D1 with a 50% error probability, this thermal noise floor causes the baseline QBER to rise naturally at long distances, even without an adversary present."*

### Why It Matters
Separates distance-induced optical attenuation from adversarial interference, a crucial distinction in QKD security operations.

## Slide 4: SPAD Detector Kinetics & Dynamics
- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/04_detector_physics_and_dead_time.png`
- **Slide Title:** *Bob's Receiver Model: SPAD Saturation, Dead Time & Afterpulsing*

### What It Shows
A 4-panel breakdown of SPAD physical dynamics:
1. Nonparalyzable dead time count rate saturation: $r_{\mathrm{obs}} = \frac{\lambda}{1 + \lambda \tau_{\mathrm{dead}}}$
2. Rate-dependent effective dead time model: $\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}(\lambda) = \tau_0 + \Delta \tau \frac{\lambda}{\lambda + \lambda_c}$
3. Exponential afterpulsing decay queue: $P(\Delta t) \propto \frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{ap}}} e^{-\Delta t / \tau_{\mathrm{ap}}}$ with dead-time suppression window
4. Temperature dependence of dark counts: $\lambda_{\mathrm{dark}}(T) = \lambda_0 [1 + \beta (T - T_{\mathrm{ref}})]$

### What to Say
> *"Real single-photon detectors are non-ideal semiconductor devices. When an avalanche occurs, the SPAD requires a finite dead time ($\tau_{\mathrm{dead}} \approx 50\,\text{ns}$) to quench and recharge. Any photon arriving during this dead interval is lost nonparalyzably. Furthermore, heavy photon bombardment causes thermal buildup, extending the effective dead time. Trapped carriers subsequently release delayed afterpulses, creating temporal correlation between successive clicks."*

### Why It Matters
Proves Bob's receiver exhibits realistic hardware non-idealities that adversaries can exploit (e.g., blinding, saturation, afterpulse timing attacks).

- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/05_basis_selection_and_sifting_funnel.png`
- **Slide Title:** *Protocol Post-Processing: Basis Sifting Funnel Reduction*

### What It Shows

2. **Right Panel:** Log-scale sifting funnel illustrating actual pulse counts from 10,000 transmitted pulses $\to$ 5,000 basis-matched pulses $\to$ ~150 detected clicks $\to$ ~75 sifted key bits $\to$ ~1 error bit.

### What to Say
> *"This figure illustrates the data reduction funnel of the BB84 protocol. Starting from 10,000 optical pulses emitted by Alice, approximately 50% are discarded during basis reconciliation because Alice and Bob picked mismatched bases. Channel attenuation and detector efficiency reduce the detected events to approximately 1.5% of total pulses, yielding 75 pristine sifted key bits with a baseline error rate of approximately 1%."*

### Why It Matters
Connects raw optical transmission directly to cryptographic key generation yield and baseline error rates.

## Slide 9: Multi-Attack Signature Profiles Across 6 Attacks
- **Figure File:** `outputs/visualizations/presentation/09_multi_attack_signatures_comparison.png`
- **Slide Title:** *Multi-Attack Physical Footprints Across Telemetry Dimensions*

### What It Shows
A grouped bar chart comparing the normalized physical deviations of all 6 implemented attacks across 5 telemetry dimensions: QBER, total count rate, decoy residual, timing jitter, and detector imbalance.

### What to Say
> *"This is perhaps the most important slide for understanding QISM's design. Notice that different attacks affect entirely different dimensions of telemetry:
> - **Intercept-Resend** dramatically elevates QBER, but leaves timing unchanged.
> - **Channel Loss** drops total counts and gains, with minimal direct QBER impact.
> - **Source Manipulation** distorts decoy state residuals.
> - **Detector Blinding** collapses count rate and induces severe detector imbalance.
> - **Timing Shifts** alter arrival jitter without immediately alerting QBER.
> If a security system only monitors QBER, it is blind to multiple classes of physical attacks."*

### Why It Matters
Provides the core motivation for multi-dimensional infrastructure telemetry monitoring.
